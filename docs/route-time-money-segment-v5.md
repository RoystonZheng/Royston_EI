# Route time and money decision model v5

This is the current specification for `route_experiment/decision.py` version `route_time_money_segment_v5_signed_fare_audit`. It defines a route decision rule and an auditable segment ledger. It does not establish full fleet profitability.

## Route decision

First exclude candidates that fail reachability, endpoint, legality, or route validation. Then union all remaining route alternatives from every method for the same task/scenario into one shared pool. Both profiles score that exact pool using the same route-time definition, money-cost scope, and currency. If any hard-feasible candidate lacks a required comparable monetary input, block both profiles for the whole scenario instead of ranking a partial subset. Lower scores are preferred:

```text
score = time_weight * minmax(travel_time_sec)
      + money_weight * minmax(net_economic_cost)
```

Both min-max normalizations use the same complete hard-feasible candidates across methods for one decision scenario. A constant dimension contributes zero. Ties break by raw travel time, raw net cost, then candidate ID. Weighted scores never compensate for a hard-constraint failure.

| Profile | Time weight | Money weight |
|---|---:|---:|
| `time_priority_0_6_0_4` | 0.6 | 0.4 |
| `money_priority_0_4_0_6` | 0.4 | 0.6 |

These time-versus-money weights are the user-approved experiment mapping. They must not be attributed to the meeting transcript as its explicit time/money weights.

## Journey-segment ledger

Keep an itemized row for each journey segment, such as vehicle-to-pickup deadhead and passenger service. Keep ordered road-Link energy details nested under the segment so its inputs remain auditable.

- **Energy:** retain signed EV v2 net kWh for every Link and segment, along with propulsion draw, regeneration, auxiliary use, distance, and time where available.
- **Electricity billing:** sum signed Link energy within the journey segment first. Then set `billable_energy_kwh = max(segment_net_energy_kwh, 0)` and calculate `electricity_cost = billable_energy_kwh * explicit_electricity_price`. Do not clip regenerative energy Link by Link; that changes the billed amount.
- **Fare revenue:** deadhead revenue is known zero. Record an observed passenger fare once on the passenger journey segment. Allocate revenue to Links only when an explicit per-kilometre fare schedule is part of the frozen scenario.
- **Net cost:** candidate money cost is priced journey-segment electricity minus known passenger fare, plus the measured API/token and compute cost for that method exactly once. Preserve known segment subtotals and leave unknown components null.
- **Fare in route alternatives:** record revenue once on each passenger-service journey segment and aggregate it once per served order. A fixed order fare that is independent of the chosen path is constant across that order's route alternatives and cancels in their ranking. Under a distance-based or Link-based fare rule, compute revenue from each candidate's passenger-segment distance using the frozen local-currency rate; same-OD alternatives can then have different revenue. Allocate revenue to individual Links only when the frozen fare contract defines that allocation. Never record deadhead revenue or duplicate a fare across Links.
- **API/token and compute expense:** store measured usage and matching rates once in the method record, separate from road-Link and journey-segment energy. Add that method cost once when evaluating each alternative route generated by that method; never allocate it to Links or sum it across alternative candidates. Missing in-scope usage or rates make the shared comparison unscoreable.
- **Unknowns:** missing fare, tariff, usage, or rate remains null. Never convert an unknown to zero. A net-cost score is blocked when required inputs for the declared scope are missing or incomparable. An operating-cost-only result must not be labeled full economic cost or profit.

The 3–4 CNY/km figures recorded in the existing transcript summary are illustrative sensitivity values, not a frozen fare. They must not be applied to Porto, NYC, or another scenario without an explicit local-currency fare basis.

## Signed fares and field-level audit

Observed passenger fare amounts must be finite but may be negative. Preserve their signed source value; a negative amount increases net cost and must be flagged for source-semantics review. Do not assume it is a refund, clamp it to zero, or discard it. Every segment reports the accounting currency, fare/electricity source basis, and a `null_reasons` map for unavailable values. API/token and compute null reasons remain on the method-level record because those costs are not allocated to road segments.

## Implementation and reporting

`route_experiment/decision.py` provides the v5 deterministic candidate scorer and the segment economics aggregator. A shared pool with one unique feasible route is recorded as a single-candidate decision and does not show that a weight changed route choice. A method-level single route remains comparable when other methods provide alternatives for that same task. Recalculation of existing results belongs in a hash-bound versioned sidecar; keep source route and dispatch outputs unchanged.

The net-cost field is a declared, priced scope rather than a full profit model. Vehicle depreciation, maintenance, insurance, taxes, tolls, and any other excluded expense must remain explicit limitations. Energy values are simulated model outputs unless supported by vehicle telemetry.
